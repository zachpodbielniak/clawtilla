* Office world packs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: world-packs
:END:

A world pack is a directory that describes a place for the office to be drawn in.  clawtilla knows nothing about any pack's geometry: everything it does asks for "a zone tagged X", so a glass office, a cozy town and -- later -- a game world are the same to it.  The overview is in [[file:office.org][office.org]].

** The layout of a pack
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: layout
:END:

#+begin_example
my-office/
├── office.yaml        the manifest (required)
├── choreography.yaml  rules that override the built-in choreography (optional)
├── map.tmj            a Tiled map, for the 2D view (one of the two renderers is required)
├── tiles.png          its tileset
└── office.rlevel      a libregnum level, for the 3D view
#+end_example

The pack's id is its directory name.  Agents with the office tools (the chief, the custodial team) write only under =<state_dir>/office/worlds/=: a shipped pack is copied there on its first change, and one of yours is never written -- they make a new world from it instead; see [[file:office-custodial-team.org][office-custodial-team.org]].  Put it under one of the world directories: =office.world_dirs=, =<state_dir>/office/worlds/=, or the shipped =data/office/worlds/=, searched in that order.  A pack id found twice keeps the first, so a copy of =modern-office= in your own directory replaces the shipped one.

** office.yaml
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: manifest
:END:

#+begin_src yaml
name: Modern Office
description: "A glassy open-plan office."
license: CC0-1.0                  # required: a pack with no licence is refused
renderers:
  2d:
    map: map.tmj                  # relative paths only, inside the pack
  3d:
    level: office.rlevel
  adapter:                        # optional: a world adapter draws it instead
    module: tiled                 # see office-adapters.org
    source: map.tmj
zones:
  - id: floor
    name: "Open floor"
    tags: [desk]
    team: core                    # optional: this team's desks first
    capacity: 12                  # optional: otherwise the anchor count
    anchors_2d:                   # seats, in map pixels
      - [400, 112]
      - [496, 112]
    anchors_3d:                   # the same seats, in level units (x, y, z)
      - [12.5, 0.0, 3.5]
      - [15.5, 0.0, 3.5]
  - id: boardroom
    tags: ["meeting:10"]          # a meeting room for ten
    anchors_2d: [[1120, 352], [1152, 352]]
districts: [north-wing, south-wing]   # optional; see "Federation" in office.org
assets:
  - { path: tiles.png, sha256: 5f3c..., source: "drawn by hand", license: CC0-1.0 }
#+end_src

Load rules (=clawt_office_world_load()=):

- *Refused*, so the pack is listed under ~refused~ by ~clawtilla office worlds~: the file is missing or not YAML; there is no =license=; =renderers= names neither a map nor a level; a renderer or asset path is absolute or contains =..=.
- *Warned* and kept: a zone with no id (ignored), a tag nothing sends anybody to (usually a typo), a zone with no anchors (nobody can stand in it), and a pack with no =lounge= (where anything without a zone of its own goes).

=clawt_office_world_verify_assets()= checks every listed asset exists and matches its =sha256=; that is the check a pack synced from another fleet has to pass.

** Zone tags
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: zone-tags
:END:

The tags something in clawtilla sends people to (=clawt_office_zone_tag_count()= / =_nth()=):

| Tag | Used for |
|-----+----------|
| =desk= | where an agent works; =team= on the zone keeps a team's desks together |
| =team_area= | a team's own corner (=team_area:<team>=) |
| =lead_office= | where team leads sit |
| =chief_office= | where the chief of staff sits |
| =lounge= | where idle agents rest; also the fallback for any missing zone |
| =breakroom= | a second idle spot, used when the lounge is full |
| =meeting= | a meeting room (=meeting:<capacity>=) |
| =town_hall= | the big room a round of every lead goes to |
| =phone_booth= | where a call is taken when there is one |
| =bug_corner= | where an agent whose turn broke stands |
| =library= | reading, searching and research |
| =lab= | running commands and tests |
| =whiteboard= | planning, tasks and delegation |
| =terminal= | driving a browser, a screen or a computer |
| =server_room= | decoration with blinking lights; optional |
| =visitor= | where remote workers (Grok bots, other fleets) appear |
| =entrance= | where ephemeral helpers arrive and leave |
| =usage_board= | the wall the plan allowances are drawn on |

A zone may carry several tags.  =meeting:4= and =meeting:10= both answer =meeting=; the director picks the smallest that fits the people in the round.

** How seats are given
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: seats
:END:

The director (=clawt_office_direct()=, served as ~office.layout~) gives every participant a seat:

1. Everybody gets a *home*: the chief the =chief_office=, leads a =lead_office=, a Grok bot a =visitor= desk, everybody else a =desk= -- their team's zone first.  Homes are given in id order, so a refresh never reshuffles the room.
2. Meetings are seated next, together, in the smallest =meeting= zone that fits (=town_hall= for a town hall).
3. Everybody else goes where the choreography sends them: home for desk work, thinking and waiting; beside the assignee's desk when delivering; the zone for their station, the lounge, the bug corner.
4. A full zone overflows to another zone with the same tag, then to standing room at its last anchor.  A tag the pack does not have falls back to the desk (for a call or work) or the lounge, and the placement says so in =fallback=.

So a pack's capacity is real: give a meeting room as many anchors as it has chairs.

** Making a 2D pack in Tiled
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: tiled
:END:

1. Start from a shipped pack: copy =data/office/worlds/modern-office= to =~/office-worlds/my-office=, and set =office.world_dirs: [~/office-worlds]= and =office.world: my-office=.
2. Open ~map.tmj~ in Tiled.  It has a =floor= layer, a =furniture= layer and a =zones= object layer whose rectangles are named for the zones, with the tags in their /type/.
3. Draw.  Keep the tileset at 32×32 tiles, or change =tilewidth= in the map and the anchors with it.
4. Write each zone into ~office.yaml~ with its anchors in map pixels: the middle of the tile somebody sits on.
5. ~clawtilla office worlds~ lists it, or says why not; ~clawtilla office layout~ shows who would sit where.

** Making a 3D pack in lrgeditor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lrgeditor
:END:

The 3D view loads a libregnum =.rlevel= (YAML).  Edit it in lrgeditor or by hand: each node has a =transform= and a =visual= (=primitive= with =cube=, =plane=, =sphere=, =cylinder=, =capsule=, =cone=, =torus=, or =mesh_asset= for a glTF/GLB/OBJ/VOX file inside the pack).  The shipped levels use one unit per map tile, with the map's x as x and its y as z, so =anchors_3d= are the 2D anchors divided by the tile size.  A camera node is optional; the renderer brings its own orbit camera.

** Restyling a pack: PixelLab and Meshy
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: restyle
:END:

Since 0.77.0 a pack can be redrawn in another style by two paid services, keeping its layout -- the map, zones, seats and anchors -- and replacing its art:

#+begin_src bash
clawtilla office api-key pixellab                     # or any agent's pixellab integration
clawtilla office api-key meshy                        # for --3d; or any agent's meshy integration
clawtilla office world restyle modern-office neon --style "cyberpunk, neon signs" --3d
clawtilla decision answer office-generate-restyle-neon:1a2b3c4d approve
clawtilla office use neon
#+end_src

- *Tiles (on by default; =--no-2d= turns them off)*: every tile kind the base map uses is redrawn by PixelLab at the map's tile size, *starting from the base pack's own tile* (=init_image=, cut from the base sheet by ~clawt_office_restyle_cut()~) -- floors and walls as flat seamless textures, furniture as one object on a transparent background, each prompt saying what the tile must not contain -- and painted into its *own slot* of a new ~tiles.png~, so ~map.tmj~ is copied unchanged.  Pixel art is scaled with nearest-neighbour, so it stays pixels.  Needs cairo (=POPPLER=1=); without it a restyle asks for =--3d= alone.  The first live restyle drew from the tile's name alone, with a style that itself said "office", and PixelLab drew a little office -- desk, window, plants -- into nearly every slot; the starting picture and the "only this one object" wording are what keep a carpet a carpet.
- *Models (=--3d=)*: every furniture kind the 3D level draws as one node -- =T<x>-<y>=, the cell the map says it is in -- is made by Meshy (=text-to-3d=, =meshy-6-lite= with a texture, about 3000 polygons, origin at the bottom, real-world size) as =models/<kind>.glb=, and every node of that kind becomes ~kind: mesh-asset~ pointing at it, stood on the floor at unit scale.  Walls, glass and doors are the building, not furniture: each wall cell is its own node, so a model would repeat cell by cell; they keep their primitives and are redrawn as tiles only.  A kind drawn from several nodes (a phone booth's back, sides and roof) keeps them too.
- *Names*: what each tile is comes from the base manifest's =tiles:= list -- one name per gid, from 1 -- which ~make office-worlds~ writes from the same enum that draws them.  A pack without one is refused, naming why; add the list to a hand-made pack to make it restylable.
- *One card first*: how many tiles (PixelLab prices each when it is done) and how many models with their total in Meshy credits.  Nothing is drawn or spent before approve.  The id is =office-generate-restyle-<name>:<short id>=.
- *Kept as it is made*: each tile and model is written to =<state_dir>/office/restyle/<name>/parts/= the moment it arrives.  A step that fails is listed and the rest carry on; asking again for the same name -- after a restart, a refusal or a removed pack -- says how many parts are already made and buys only the rest.
- *Assembled, then checked, then shown*: the base is copied whole into a hidden directory, its sheet, level, models, ~provenance.yaml~ (which job made each kind, and whose terms apply) and manifest replaced, and only a pack that loads with every checksum holding is renamed into =<state_dir>/office/worlds/<name>/=.  Its licence names where the art came from -- never the base's CC0, because the new art is the services' to license.  Nothing switches to it until somebody chooses it.
- *Who asked*: the custodial team can ask with ~clawtilla_office_world_restyle~ (gated by ~tools.office~); the card goes to the operator, and the outcome is said in the operator's chat with the agent that asked.

** Characters
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: characters
:END:

A participant is drawn as a body in its agent's colour with its generated face (or picture) as the head.  Per-agent characters made by the custodial team's asset steward live in =<state_dir>/office/characters/<agent>/= and are used when present; see [[file:office.org][office.org]].

** Licensing and provenance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: licensing
:END:

- A pack has one =license=; an asset with a different one says so in its own =license=.
- =source= says where an asset came from: a URL, an author, or a generator and the model it used.  A generated asset also records the generator's terms; see [[file:office.org][office.org]] and the custodial team.
- Only ship what you may redistribute.  LimeZu's interiors, for example, are not redistributable even when bought; a pack using them is a private pack.

** The shipped packs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: shipped
:END:

=modern-office= and =cozy-town= are drawn entirely by =tools/clawt-office-genworld.c= -- the tiles with cairo, the 3D level from primitives -- so they are CC0 and nothing is downloaded.  ~make office-worlds~ redraws them (it needs =cairo-devel= and =json-glib-devel=; a normal build does not); the output is deterministic, so the sha256 sums in their manifests only change when the drawing does.  The program is also the worked example of describing a world as rooms: a floor, a wall style, the zone the room is and its furniture, with the seats coming from the furniture.
